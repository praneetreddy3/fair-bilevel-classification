% 1. ABSTRACT -- replace the whole abstract body
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Collaborative learning across institutions must often satisfy both privacy and group-fairness requirements while clients hold small, class-imbalanced local datasets. We propose a client-centric bilevel framework in which clients share only synthetic data. Each client draws jointly $(S,Y)$-stratified minibatches from its private data, trains an inner classifier on a synthetic batch with matching stratum labels, optionally regularized by Universum points to counter class imbalance, and updates the synthetic features with an Augmented Lagrangian (AL) that penalizes a bounded, sigmoid-smoothed Equal Opportunity (EO) surrogate evaluated on the original minibatch. Hypergradients are computed by implicit differentiation with conjugate-gradient Hessian-vector products, so no raw records or gradients leave the client. An optional differential-privacy stage can perturb the released synthetics, and we quantify its privacy budget. The server aggregates the synthetic batches over multiple rounds and trains a global predictor. With linear logistic regression on Credit, Adult, Law, and COMPAS, the released synthetic data reduce the EO gap relative to training on pooled real data where baseline disparity is large (Law: $0.381\to0.262$; COMPAS: $0.276\to0.166$) at a moderate accuracy cost. A control with the AL penalty disabled attains similar gaps, indicating that most of this gain comes from stratified synthetic sharing rather than the penalty itself. Compared with FedAvg, FairFed, FedFB, and FedFair, which reach higher accuracy and lower EO gaps on several datasets but exchange model updates and real-data group statistics, our method trades some utility for synthetic-only sharing.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% 2. CONTRIBUTIONS -- replace the three \item lines
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\item \textbf{Framework:} A client-centric bilevel procedure that draws jointly $(S,Y)$-stratified minibatches, trains on synthetic batches with matching stratum labels, optionally adds Universum regularization against class imbalance, and shares only synthetic data with the server.
\item \textbf{Local solver:} An Augmented Lagrangian outer update of the synthetic features on a bounded EO surrogate, with implicit hypergradients computed by conjugate gradient on Hessian-vector products, run entirely on the client so that raw records never leave it.
\item \textbf{Empirical study:} An evaluation on four datasets with a $\rho{=}0$ control that isolates the effect of the AL penalty, a Universum ablation, a comparison with FedAvg, FairFed, FedFB, and FedFair under matched splits, the privacy budget of the optional DP release, and local-vs-pooled EO under non-IID partitions.

\subsection{Practical Guidance}
Because a $\rho{=}0$ control attains EO gaps similar to the penalized runs, practitioners can first run the synthetic pipeline without the penalty and enable the AL stage only if the validation EO gap remains above the target.
Monitor DemP and EOD alongside EO, because an EO win can coexist with a DemP change.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
% 8. CONCLUSION -- replace from "Under linear logistic regression, ..." to the end of the paragraph
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Under linear logistic regression, the released synthetic data yield the largest EO gains over real-data training on Law and COMPAS; a $\rho{=}0$ control indicates these come mainly from stratified synthetic sharing, and fair federated baselines that exchange model updates reach higher utility at the cost of sharing more information. Future work includes making the EO penalty itself more effective (e.g., a less saturating surrogate), a noise-level sweep for the privacy release, and $S$-blind predictors.
